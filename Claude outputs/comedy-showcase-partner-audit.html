Ann Arbor Comedy Showcase: what the app actually does today

An audit of the live partner onboarding, Date Pass redemption and billing code — followed by a plain-English setup guide written for Claudia and her box office manager.

4 September 2026 Audited against main, 25 migrations through 20260903140000
The short answer

The product is built. The plumbing behind the money has never been switched on.

Everything Claudia and her box office would touch — registration, the Date Spot, the offer editor, staff logins, the scanner, the valid/invalid screens, the redemption ledger — exists and works as described. Roles are real, the scanner is a real page a box office phone can install, and staff genuinely cannot see billing or customer data.

What is not proven is the last mile: Stripe has never been configured or run end to end, the metering job that turns redemptions into an invoice has to be created by hand, and no invitation email is ever sent to a staff member you add. There are five things below I'd fix or confirm before Claudia opens a browser.

Recommendation: send her the guide as “here's how it works,” but don't walk her through Add a card until you've done one full dry run with a throwaway business. Details in Recommended fixes.

The exact sequence

  1. Claudia opens /partners and presses “Become a Partner.”Lands on /partners/join. Two fields: her name and an email. No .edu required — the partner side deliberately allows any address.
  2. A one-time code is emailed to her.Passwordless. Supabase sends the code (via Resend for deliverability); she types it and she's in. That code is the whole of “email verification” — there is no separate confirm-your-email step.
  3. She fills in seven steps, and each one saves as she goes.Closing the laptop halfway loses the step she was on, not the four before it.
  4. Submitting sets the business to pending.She lands on the dashboard with a “Pending review” pill and a banner reading “With us for review.” Everything is editable; students just can't see the venue yet.
  5. You approve it in Backstage → Partners.Manual step partner_is_live() returns true only once a human sets the status to active. Rejecting requires a note, which is shown to them verbatim.
  6. Only when she wants an offer live does money appear.Offers → “Save and turn it on” is blocked until a card is on file. Billing → “Add a card” redirects to a Stripe-hosted page; card details never touch the Loose Leaf app.

Audit 2

What a redemption actually is

Before they arrive

  • By default an offer is marked “Only for two people planning a date” (requires_date defaults to true). The pass unlocks from inside a chat between two people who matched — not from browsing. If the venue unticks it, it can also be unlocked from Campus → Date Spots.
  • Unlocking calls issue_date_pass(), which mints a code in the format LL-XXXX-XXXX. The alphabet deliberately excludes O I L 0 1, so nothing is ambiguous read aloud at a counter.
  • The pass expires after the offer's pass lifetime (default 14 days) and lives in the student's wallet at /app/passes, reached from their profile.
  • Asking twice hands back the same pass rather than minting a second one.

What's on the customer's phone

A dark ticket-shaped card: venue name, the offer in large type, the days it's valid, the expiry date, a QR code, the code printed underneath it, and the line “Show this to your server.” Any terms the venue wrote appear as small print at the bottom.

What the employee does

  1. Opens the scanner./partners/dashboard/scan — installed on the home screen it opens straight here. Three ways in: tap Open the camera, type the code, or point the phone's own camera at the QR (the QR encodes a link back to this page with the code pre-filled).
  2. The code is checked, read-only.partner_lookup_pass(). Nothing has happened yet. Green tick + “Valid Loose Leaf Date Pass”, the offer in 28px type, the terms, and Status · Unused. Or a red cross with one of four plain sentences: “Already used.” · “This pass has expired.” · “This pass was cancelled.” · “This offer isn't running right now.”
  3. Presses Confirm redemption.The screen warns underneath: “Press this once they're actually here. It can't be undone.”
  4. The database does the work under a row lock.redeem_date_pass() re-checks staff permission and the account's billing state, re-checks void / used / expired / not-running, writes a row to date_pass_redemptions with the $1.50 fee stamped onto it, flips the pass to redeemed, and logs a verified_date event.
  5. Full-screen green confirmation.A large tick, “Date verified ✓”, and “Give them the discount. This is now counted in your dashboard.” Plus a Scan another button.

How a second scan is prevented

LayerWhat stops it
The same pass, twiceThe pass row is locked (for update) and its status becomes redeemed. A second scan returns “Already used.” before the confirm button ever appears. Overridable per offer with “Let one pass be used more than once” — off by default.
The same person, again next weekPer-offer rule: once ever / once every N days (default 30) / as often as they like. The clock runs from the visit, not the unlock.
Too many overallPer-month, per-day and lifetime caps on the offer. Hitting the monthly cap stops the offer being handed out until the 1st.
Two racing devicesThe row lock. One wins, the other reads “Already used.”

Where it shows up afterwards

  • Redemptions — a ledger row: time, offer name, the last four characters of the code, and nothing that could become a person. No names, ever, by construction.
  • Overview / Analytics — today, this week, this month; and a funnel from “shown to a student” through to “walked in.”
  • Billing — this month's count × $1.50, last month's total, and a credit meter.
The customer's phone does not update live. Nothing pushes to it. Next time they open Date Passes the card is greyed out with “Already used.” In a box office queue this can produce a “…did that work?” beat. The employee's green screen is the only confirmation in the room.

Two rules are structural, not settings: scanning can never be revoked from a team member, and Settings can never be granted to one.

The gap that matters for a box office: no invitation email is sent. Adding boxoffice@… in Team creates the invitation and then nothing happens — the invitee is never told. Somebody has to tell them out loud, or by their own email, that they should go to /partners/login and enter that exact address. On first sign-in they see a “Ann Arbor Comedy Showcase added you → Accept” screen. Invitations expire after 14 days.

The second consequence: signing in on a new device sends a code to that address, so whoever holds that inbox has to be reachable. For a rotating box office, a shared address the whole team can open — boxoffice@a2comedy.com — is the configuration that actually survives contact with a Thursday night.

So the ideal you described — open this page → scan → valid/invalid → apply the discount — is what the app already does, with one asterisk: the page lives behind a login, and getting that login onto each device is the setup cost.

Can they do “two discounted tickets”?

Yes, but the “two” lives in words, not in the system. There is no quantity field. The clean expressions available today:

  • “Buy one, get one” — the native type, and the closest thing to a two-ticket deal the scanner will render on its own.
  • “Dollars off” with terms reading “Valid on two admissions to the same show. One pass per couple.”
  • “A couple package” with a description like “Two tickets to any Thursday show, $20.”
Worth saying to Claudia out loud: a Date Pass is issued to one student, and the system has no idea whether one person or two walked in. Whatever “two tickets” means operationally has to be written into the terms — the software will not enforce it.

The fee structure: built vs. switched on

A · What the code actually implements

Invoiced monthly in arrears via a $0/month Stripe subscription used purely as an invoicing container (so you get Smart Retries, dunning and portal history)partner-billing-setupNever run live

B · What is still manual or unproven

  • Nothing has ever run against real Stripe. The meter, the metered price, the webhook endpoint, the customer portal config and the retry policy all have to be created by hand in the Stripe dashboard. The runbook is docs/STRIPE-PAY-PER-REDEMPTION.md; it has never been executed.
  • The metering job has to be scheduled by hand (pg_cron, or any external scheduler). If it doesn't exist, redemptions sit at bill_status = 'pending' forever — recorded correctly, invoiced never. The venue would get the discounts and no bill.
  • Approving a partner is you, in Backstage. Not automated, and shouldn't be.
  • The new-partner credit limit is $25. At $1.50 each that is 16 redemptions, then a $10 grace band (≈6 more already-issued passes honoured). After that, new passes silently stop going out until an invoice is paid — and there is no Backstage UI to raise it, only SQL.
So the sentence you want to send is true — “no monthly fee, no upfront cost, $1.50 only when a Date Pass is successfully redeemed, billed monthly” — provided you finish the Stripe configuration before the first scan. The product genuinely charges nothing else; the risk is that it charges nothing at all.
Audit 6

Recommended fixes before onboarding Comedy Showcase

Critical before launch

Stripe has never been configured or tested end to end

#1

Two failure modes, both landing on Claudia. If partner-billing-setup isn't deployed with live keys, “Add a card” errors out at the exact moment she's trying to turn the offer on. If the metering job isn't scheduled, every redemption is recorded and never billed.

Fix: run the whole runbook against a throwaway partner — register, approve, add a card, issue a pass, scan it, force a meter run, confirm an invoice appears in Stripe — before Claudia touches it.

Invitation emails are not sent

#2

Claudia adds the box office address and nothing arrives. She has no way of knowing that, and the employee has no way of knowing they were added. Everything about the staff flow assumes somebody says it out loud.

Fix: ship the invitation email (Resend is already wired for auth mail), or — as a stopgap — the guide below makes the hand-off explicit and step 8 tells her exactly what to say. The guide compensates; it doesn't repair it.

$25 credit limit is ~16 redemptions

#3

A comedy venue with two shows a week could pass that in the first fortnight. New passes then stop being issued mid-month, invisibly from their side — the offer just quietly disappears from students' recommendations.

Fix: raise the new tier default, or set a per-partner override for the Comedy Showcase before they go live. It's a one-line SQL update today.

Confirm production is actually serving live mode

#4

If VITE_DATA_MODE isn't supabase on the deployed site, /partners/join shows a page reading “This part needs the real Loose Leaf” — a demo-mode notice with setup instructions in it. That's what Claudia would see.

Fix: open hellolooseleaf.com/partners/join in a private window and confirm you get the name-and-email form.

Confirm the Ann Arbor campus is open

#5

A campus opens at 50 finished profiles; below that students land on a waitlist. An approved venue with a live offer that no student can unlock is a worse outcome than not signing them yet.

Fix: check the count, and set Claudia's expectations about volume in the first month in the email rather than letting the dashboard tell her.

Should improve

The QR deep-link is lost if the device isn't signed in

#6

The pass QR encodes /partners/dashboard/scan?code=LL-…. On a signed-in device that's lovely — point the native camera, land on a checked pass. On a signed-out one the layout redirects to /partners/login with replace, dropping the code. The employee then logs in and finds an empty scanner.

Fix: stash the code through the login redirect and re-apply it after sign-in.

Nothing confirms the redemption on the customer's side

#7

No live update, no toast, no push. In a queue the couple has only the employee's word. A small “this pass was just redeemed” state on the student's card would remove a whole class of awkward counter conversation.

The onboarding offer step can't describe a package

#8

“A couple package” and “Something else” both need a description — the dashboard offer sheet has that field, the onboarding wizard's version doesn't, and doesn't save it. Pick either of those two during signup and the scanner falls back to showing the title.

Fix: add the field to the wizard. Until then, the guide below routes Claudia to build the offer from the dashboard, which sidesteps it.

“Off a minimum spend” shows the wrong thing at the counter

#9

partner_lookup_pass() has no case for spend_threshold, so it falls through to the title. The dashboard says “$5 off $30+”; the employee's screen says “Weeknight Date.” The counter screen is the one place the wording has to be exact.

The Redemptions ledger's amount column is always empty

#10

redeem_date_pass() accepts a ticket amount; the scanner never passes one. Every row reads “—”. Either capture it (one optional field on the confirm screen) or drop the column — an empty column reads as broken data.

“New customers only” is a control that does nothing

#11

It's on the offer form, it's saved to the column, and no function anywhere reads it. A venue could tick it, believe it's enforced, and be wrong.

Fix: implement it or hide it. Don't ship a checkbox that lies.

Nice to have

A short scanner URL

#12

/partners/dashboard/scan is three words longer than it needs to be and contains “dashboard,” which is exactly what you're telling staff they don't need. A /scan alias reads better on a printed card and out loud.

A Backstage screen for credit limits

#13

staff_set_partner_credit() exists; nothing calls it. Raising a venue's ceiling means opening the SQL editor.

Nothing has been tested on a physical iPhone

#14

The box office phone is very likely an iPhone. The QR decoder, the home-screen install and the camera path are all verified only in headless Chromium, and two of three past install bugs were found in seconds on a real handset and by nothing in a test suite.

Getting the Comedy Showcase on Loose Leaf

Everything below is written to be forwarded to whoever runs your box office. It doesn't assume any technical knowledge, and the part your staff have to learn is five steps long.

How the partnership works

Loose Leaf is a free dating app for the University of Michigan campus. When two students match and start planning a date, the app suggests real local places to go — and where a place has an offer for Loose Leaf couples, the couple can unlock a Date Pass: a ticket on their phone with a QR code and a short reference number.

They bring it to your box office. Your employee scans it, sees a green valid screen with your offer written on it, and applies the discount. That's the whole product.

There is no cost to be listed. No setup fee, no monthly fee, no advertising spend. Loose Leaf charges $1.50 only when one of your staff actually scans a pass, and invoices that once at the end of the month. A month with no redemptions costs nothing.

Step 1 — Setting up (Claudia, about 15 minutes, once)

  1. Go to hellolooseleaf.com/partners and press “Become a Partner.”Enter your name and the email address you actually check. You'll get a short code by email — type it in. There is no password to create or remember, ever.
  2. Fill in the seven-step form about the Showcase.Name, address, what kind of night out you are, a couple of photos, your hours. When it asks whether you own or manage the venue, answer honestly — either way you get full control of the account. It saves as you go, so you can stop and come back.
  3. Skip the “Offer” step for now and submit.You'll build the offer properly from your dashboard in a moment, where there are more options. Submitting sends the Showcase to us for review — a person reads every application, usually within a day.
  4. We approve you, and the Showcase appears on the app.You'll be able to see everything in your dashboard before that; students just can't see you until we've said yes.
  5. Create your offer: Offers → New offer.See “Choosing your offer” below for what you can set.
  6. Add a card: Billing → Add a card.This happens on Stripe's own secure page — Loose Leaf never sees the number. Nothing is charged for having a card on file. It's needed only so we can invoice you for redemptions that actually happen.
  7. Turn the offer on: Offers → “Save and turn it on.”It's in front of couples straight away. You can pause it any time, from any device.

Step 2 — Setting up the box office (once, about 5 minutes)

The simplest setup, and the one we'd recommend for a rotating box office: create one Loose Leaf login for the box office rather than one per employee, using a shared address the whole team can reach — something like boxoffice@a2comedy.com.

  1. In your dashboard, go to Team → Add someone.Enter the box office email address and choose the role Staff. A Staff login can do exactly one thing: scan a pass. It cannot see your billing, your analytics, your offers, or anything at all about the customers.
  2. Tell your box office manager directly that you've done it.We don't email them automatically yet, so this bit is on you — a message saying “I've added boxoffice@a2comedy.com to Loose Leaf, go to hellolooseleaf.com/partners/login and log in with it” is all it takes.
  3. On the box office phone or tablet, open hellolooseleaf.com/partners/login.Enter that address, get the code from the inbox, type it in, and press Accept on the welcome screen. The scanner opens.
  4. Add it to the home screen.Tap Install (or Add to home screen) at the top of that screen and follow the steps — it knows what kind of phone you're on. You get an app icon called Loose Leaf Scanner that opens straight to the camera. No hunting for a browser tab mid-rush.
  5. Repeat on any other device you want to scan from.You can use as many phones, tablets or computers as you like, at the same time, all on that one login. It stays signed in — nobody has to log in again at the start of a shift.
  6. Optional: Team → Print a counter card.One printed sheet with a QR code. Anyone who scans it gets the setup steps for their own phone. Tape it by the till.

If you'd rather give people their own logins, you can — add each employee's email as Staff, and each one gets the same scanner-only screen. It's the same amount of work per person, so it's a question of whether you want redemptions attributable to individuals.

When a Loose Leaf customer arrives

At the box office · 5–15 seconds
  1. The customer shows you a dark Date Pass on their phone, with a QR code and a code like LL-4KHD-9RTM.
  2. Open the Loose Leaf Scanner on the box office phone.
  3. Tap Open the camera and hold their code in the square — or just type the code in the box underneath.
  4. A green screen shows your offer in large type. Tap Confirm redemption.
  5. It says “Date verified ✓”. Apply the discount. Done — we count it automatically.

If it's not valid you get a red screen instead, saying exactly why: already used, expired, cancelled, or this offer isn't running right now. Nothing to interpret, and nothing you need to check anywhere else.

Two small things worth knowing: the codes never contain the letters O, I or L or the digits 0 and 1, so they're unambiguous read out loud; and once a pass is confirmed it cannot be scanned again.

Choosing your offer

You haven't decided yet, and you don't have to before signing up — the offer is edited from your dashboard whenever you like. Here's what's available.

The shape of the deal. A percentage off; a dollar amount off; something free; buy-one-get-one; a set amount off a minimum spend; a “couple package” you describe in your own words; or anything else you write yourself.

Two discounted tickets works. The neatest options for a comedy room are buy one, get one, or a dollar amount off with the terms reading “valid on two admissions to the same show.” Worth knowing: the pass is issued to one person, and the app can't tell how many people walk in — so whatever “two tickets” means for you needs to be written in the terms your staff can see on screen.

When it runs. Specific days of the week (a Sunday–Thursday offer never appears on a Friday), a time window like 4 PM to close, and a start and end date if it's a limited run.

How many. A cap per month, per day, or in total. Once a cap is reached the offer simply stops being handed out until it resets — you're never surprised by volume.

How often one person can use it. Once ever, once every so many days (30 by default), or as often as they like. This counts from the visit, not from unlocking — a pass nobody brought in costs you nothing.

Who it's for. By default an offer only unlocks for two people who matched and are planning a date, so what walks in is a date rather than somebody who came for a discount. You can loosen that if you'd rather.

Small print. A short terms line that appears both on the customer's pass and on your employee's screen when they scan it — so the person applying the discount is reading your rules, not remembering them.

Pricing

No monthly fee. No upfront cost. Loose Leaf charges $1.50 only when a Date Pass is successfully redeemed.

Redemptions are counted automatically as your staff scan them, and billed once at the end of the month. A month where nobody came in costs nothing. Being listed, being recommended, your photos, your hours and your dashboard are all free and always will be.
